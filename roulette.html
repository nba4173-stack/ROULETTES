ROUND 04 · CHANCE VS. OBSERVATION

Theoretical probabilities

European roulette has 37 equally likely pockets: 0 through 36.

Red pocket18 / 37 = 48.65%

Probability of red on one fair spin.

Black pocket18 / 37 = 48.65%

Probability of black on one fair spin.

Green zero1 / 37 = 2.70%

Probability of landing on zero.

These are theoretical probabilities for one spin, assuming a fair European wheel. Your ten picks are chosen by you, so their color percentages are experimental summaries of your choices, not a random sample from actual wheel spins. In real independent spins, past outcomes do not change the next spin's probabilities.

BEHIND THE GAME

A little roulette history

Roulette developed in France during the 18th century. The name comes from the French word for “little wheel.” The mathematician Blaise Pascal is often connected to roulette's early history because his 17th-century experiments explored perpetual motion, but it would be inaccurate to call him the confirmed sole inventor of the modern game. Roulette evolved over time from several wheel and gaming traditions. The single-zero European layout became distinct from the double-zero American layout, which has an additional green 00 pocket. NumberQuest borrows the familiar 0–36 layout as a visual and educational theme—there is no wheel and no betting.

Learning note: “founder” is uncertain in roulette history. Pascal is associated with an early precursor, not definitively established as the inventor of today's roulette.
